# Yemen Warns of Growing Houthi Threat to Red Sea Security

By Syeda Manal Tirmizi

UNITED NATIONS, September 11, 2026: Yemen has warned the United Nations Security Council that the Houthi group is intensifying its military activities and seeking to establish what it described as a "new reality" in the strategically important Red Sea, raising concerns over maritime security, regional stability and the safety of international shipping through the Bab al-Mandeb Strait.

Yemen's UN envoy Abdullah al-Saadi made the remarks during [an emergency meeting of the Security Council](https://www.securitycouncilreport.org/whatsinblue/2026/09/yemen-emergency-briefing.php) on September 10 convened amid growing concerns over Houthi attacks and military movements in the region, emphasizing that the group's continued activities pose a direct threat to navigation through one of the world's most important maritime corridors.

Abdullah al-Saadi said Yemen's internationally recognized government remained committed to protecting the Bab al-Mandeb Strait, describing the waterway as a matter of international and regional security because of its importance to commercial shipping and the movement of energy and other essential goods. He urged the Security Council to respond firmly to the Houthi escalation and take steps to prevent the group from expanding its military capabilities and influence around the strategic waterway.

The Yemeni envoy called on the international community to condemn the Houthi military escalation and enforce the existing arms embargo against the group, arguing that stronger international action was necessary to prevent further deterioration of the security situation. He also maintained that Yemen's internationally recognized government had continued to exercise restraint and had sought a peaceful resolution to the conflict while carrying out its responsibility to protect the country's population and state institutions.

## Fighting Along the Western Coast

The warning comes as fighting between Yemeni government forces and the Houthis has intensified along the country's western coastline overlooking the Red Sea, an area of particular strategic significance because of its proximity to the Bab al-Mandeb Strait. The [Associated Press reported](https://apnews.com/article/e4e799701b382799a955969c212800ea) that Houthi forces captured Mokha on Thursday, adding to concerns about the security of the coast.

Yemeni Presidential Leadership Council Chairman Rashad al-Alimi has warned about the consequences of increased military activity around Bab al-Mandeb. Separately, Foreign Minister Afrah al-Zouba told the United States charge d'affaires that strengthening Yemen's state institutions and their ability to protect its coastlines, ports and territorial waters was essential to maritime security, according to [the official Saba news agency](https://www.sabanew.net/story/en/152193).

The latest developments have renewed international attention on the security of the Red Sea, particularly as the Bab al-Mandeb Strait serves as a critical connection between the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden and provides an important route for global commercial shipping. Any sustained disruption in the area could therefore create wider consequences for regional trade and international supply chains.

The renewed fighting represents a significant deterioration after several years of relative calm on some fronts, with government and Houthi forces now facing increased tensions across multiple areas. Yemen has remained embroiled in conflict since the Houthis captured the capital Sanaa and large parts of the country in September 2014, prompting a Saudi-led military intervention in March 2015 in support of the internationally recognized government.

As military activity continues, Yemen is urging the international community to strengthen diplomatic and security efforts aimed at preventing further escalation and protecting the Red Sea and Bab al-Mandeb from becoming a wider arena of regional confrontation.
