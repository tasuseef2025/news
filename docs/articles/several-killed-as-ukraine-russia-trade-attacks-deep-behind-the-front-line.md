# At Least Eight Reported Killed as Russia and Ukraine Trade Attacks

By Novexa News Desk

July 23, 2026: Reports from Ukrainian and Russian authorities described at least eight deaths in separate attacks across Ukraine, Russia and Russian-occupied Crimea, as the war continued to affect communities beyond the main battle lines.

The reported toll comprised three people in Pavlohrad, one in Ukraine's Sumy region, a child in Russia's Voronezh region, one person in Belgorod and two in Crimea. These figures came from separate local accounts of attacks on July 22 and 23, rather than a single independently verified casualty assessment.

## Three Killed in Pavlohrad

A Russian strike hit a food industry business in Pavlohrad on July 23, killing three people and starting a fire, according to Dnipropetrovsk regional authorities. [Ukrainska Pravda, citing the regional administration and prosecutors](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/07/23/8045495/), reported that the number of injured later rose to 19, including a two-year-old girl.

Initial accounts had put the number of wounded at 10. Regional head Oleksandr Hanzha subsequently announced the higher figure and said those injured were receiving treatment in hospital.

Ukrainian prosecutors said a guided aerial bomb struck the civilian business at approximately 11:20. They opened a war-crimes investigation and were documenting damage at the site. Those statements describe the authorities' initial findings, not a completed judicial determination.

In the northeastern Sumy region, a 57-year-old man died after mortar fire struck Seredyna-Buda on the evening of July 22. [Local outlet Dancor, citing the regional prosecutor's office](https://dancor.sumy.ua/news/newsline/586399), reported that he was wounded outside near the impact site and later died in hospital. An apartment building was also damaged.

## Deaths Reported in Russia and Occupied Crimea

In Voronezh, regional Governor Alexander Gusev said falling drone debris caused a house fire that killed a three-year-old boy. Buildings and the roof of a warehouse were also damaged, according to [The Moscow Times' July 23 report](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/07/23/3-killed-as-wave-of-ukrainian-drones-strikes-southern-russia-and-crimea-a93316).

Authorities in Russia's Belgorod region separately reported that a drone struck a vehicle, killing one person and injuring another. [Deutsche Welle's account of the Russian statements](https://amp.dw.com/es/tres-muertos-y-al-menos-ocho-heridos-en-rusia-tras-ataques-nocturnos-de-drones-ucranianos/a-78079479) said the wounded person was taken to hospital.

In Crimea, the Ukrainian peninsula Russia seized and annexed in 2014, Moscow-installed leader Sergei Aksyonov initially reported one death and four injuries. The Moscow Times subsequently reported his announcement of a second death. This revision explains why early reports carried lower totals for the attacks.

## Strikes Extend Beyond the Battle Lines

An energy facility in Russia's Ulyanovsk region also caught fire after a drone attack, the regional governor said. Russia's Defence Ministry claimed its air defences intercepted 223 Ukrainian drones overnight between July 22 and 23. That interception figure was a Russian military claim and was not an independently confirmed measure of the full attack.

The locations and damage described by local authorities show the geographical spread of the reported strikes, from a Ukrainian food business and residential areas to facilities inside Russia. They do not, by themselves, establish every intended target or the circumstances of each civilian casualty.

The death and injury figures in this report relate to those July attacks. They should not be read as a current total for the wider war, and differences between early accounts and subsequent local updates remain important when comparing the reports.
