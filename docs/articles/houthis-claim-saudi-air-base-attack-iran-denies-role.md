# Houthis Claim Saudi Air Base Attack as Iran Denies Role in Yemen War

By Syeda Manal Tirmizi

September 14, 2026: Yemen's Houthis said they launched missiles and drones at King Khalid Air Base in southern Saudi Arabia on Monday, as Iran denied directing the group's actions and renewed fighting put further pressure on regional diplomacy.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the attack targeted military facilities at the base in Khamis Mushait. The group claimed it had used dozens of missiles and drones against aircraft hangars, radar installations and other infrastructure. The extent of any damage was not independently established in the reports available at the time.

Saudi Civil Defence issued alerts in Khamis Mushait and neighbouring Abha. A resident of Khamis Mushait told AFP that blasts were followed by prolonged fires, but that account did not establish the condition of the air base or substantiate the group's wider claims.

## Iran Rejects Direct Involvement

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei said Tehran did not interfere in Yemen's internal affairs and described the Houthis as making their own decisions. His remarks came amid renewed scrutiny of Iran's relationship with the group, which has long received Iranian support.

Iran's denial and the Houthi account were reported by [AFP](https://novanews.co.za/houthis-target-saudi-base-as-iran-denies-involvement-in-yemen-war/). The distinction between Iranian support and responsibility for a particular operation remains important: the attack announcement itself did not independently establish who directed it.

## Regional Talks Postponed

A meeting planned in Oman between Iran and Gulf countries to discuss the Strait of Hormuz was postponed. Omani Foreign Minister Badr Albusaidi said the delay was intended to support consensus, while Tehran blamed Saudi Arabia for requesting it, according to [Reuters](https://gvwire.com/2026/09/14/gulf-states-call-off-meeting-with-iran-houthis-launch-new-attack-on-saudi/).

Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman also met US Central Command chief Admiral Brad Cooper in Jeddah on Monday. Saudi state media said their discussions covered regional developments.

The diplomatic contacts took place as the Houthis' advances along Yemen's western coast raised concerns over access to the Bab al-Mandab Strait, a key passage between the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden. Those concerns are separate from the dispute over navigation through Hormuz.

## Fighting Adds to Displacement

Yemen's internationally recognised government, backed by Saudi Arabia, said its forces had regained positions in Taiz province. Battlefield gains reported by the opposing sides remained claims, rather than independently confirmed measures of control.

The humanitarian impact was already substantial. AFP reported that the International Organization for Migration put displacement at nearly 86,000 people in its Sunday update, with more than 2,000 people having crossed to Djibouti. The figures underline the consequences for civilians as fighting expands and efforts to restart regional talks face further delays.
