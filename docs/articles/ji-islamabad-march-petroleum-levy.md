# JI Says Islamabad March Will Arrive September 23-24

By Syeda Manal Tirmizi

ISLAMABAD, September 19, 2026: Jamaat-e-Islami chief Hafiz Naeemur Rehman says the party's long march against the petroleum levy will begin with a train journey from Karachi on September 20 and reach Islamabad on September 23 and 24.

The updated timetable adds detail to JI's earlier announcement of a September 20 mobilisation. Rehman said supporters would start moving toward the capital from different parts of the country on that date, while the main convoys would arrive over the following days.

Addressing a press conference in Islamabad, he said the party had asked the authorities for permission and did not intend to block public roads. He presented abolition of the petroleum levy as JI's central demand and said the march was intended to press for economic relief.

According to [Geo News](https://www.geo.tv/latest/682594-jis-long-march-to-reach-islamabad-on-sept-23-24-says-hafiz-naeem), the train march will leave Karachi on September 20 and travel through Multan and Lahore before continuing toward Islamabad. Other participants are expected from Quetta, Chitral, Kalam, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Punjab and additional areas.

## Levy Remains the Main Dispute

JI has been campaigning against the petroleum levy through sit-ins, public meetings and negotiations with the federal government. Rehman argues that reducing or abolishing the levy would provide broad relief because fuel costs affect public transport, freight, food distribution and household budgets.

The government, by contrast, has chosen targeted support for selected consumers. Its special fuel-relief programme offers assistance calculated at Rs100 per litre to eligible users of motorcycles, rickshaws, Qingqi vehicles and cars with engines up to 800cc, subject to monthly limits and verification requirements.

Rehman has rejected that programme as an inadequate substitute for a levy reduction. He said relief should be treated as a right rather than charity and criticised the scheme for not addressing diesel, which is widely used in freight, agriculture and commercial transport.

The disagreement therefore concerns both the amount of support and how it should be delivered. The government says targeted assistance directs limited funds toward smaller vehicles and lower-income users. JI says lowering the levy would reach consumers more broadly and reduce the indirect cost passed through supply chains.

## Talks Have Not Ended the Protest

The party says four rounds of discussions have not produced implementation of its principal demand. Earlier meetings covered the petroleum levy, power-sector costs and proposals for consumer relief, but JI continued its protest campaign after concluding that the government's response did not go far enough.

In its own account of the September 18 announcement, [Jamaat-e-Islami said](https://jamaat.org/en/news/ji-proposals-could-save-rs3-4-trillion-says-hafiz-naeem-ur-rehman) the march would begin on September 20 and linked its economic proposals to potential savings in public expenditure. Claims about the amount that could be saved remain the party's estimates rather than independently verified government projections.

Rehman also said the party was willing to resume talks if the government had a workable mechanism for providing wider relief. That leaves space for further negotiations even as transport and mobilisation plans continue.

The JI chief addressed concerns raised by students scheduled to sit the Medical and Dental College Admission Test, saying the party wanted the examination to proceed and would not create an obstruction. The assurance will be tested against traffic conditions as convoys approach Islamabad.

## A Crowded Protest Calendar

The JI mobilisation comes shortly before Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf's planned September 27 march. The Islamabad High Court has ruled that peaceful protest remains constitutionally protected but cannot be conducted by occupying roads and public places in a way that blocks movement, trade, education or medical access.

Authorities are consequently expected to assess JI's permission request, routes and gathering arrangements against the same public-order framework. Rehman's statement that the party will not block roads may become central to negotiations over a venue and traffic plan.

The next key developments are whether Islamabad's administration grants permission, what route is approved for the arriving groups and whether another round of government-JI talks occurs before the march reaches the capital.

The revised timetable also clarifies that September 20 is the start of a phased journey rather than the single date on which all participants are expected in Islamabad. For residents, travellers and students, the September 23-24 arrival window is now the more important period for possible traffic and security measures.

JI's campaign has kept the petroleum levy at the centre of the political debate by connecting a fiscal instrument to everyday transport and living costs. Whether the march changes government policy will depend on turnout, negotiations and the party's ability to conduct the mobilisation within the legal conditions governing assemblies in the capital.
