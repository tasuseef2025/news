# Houthis Claim Missile and Drone Attacks on Riyadh and Yanbu

By Syeda Manal Tirmizi

Yemen's Houthi movement has claimed a combined missile and drone operation against targets in Riyadh and the Saudi port city of Yanbu, while Saudi authorities say their forces intercepted an attempted ballistic missile strike on the capital and thwarted other attacks.

The claims and official statements mark a significant expansion of the latest regional escalation. Saudi Arabia said the ballistic missile aimed at Riyadh was intercepted, with no casualties or damage immediately reported. It was the first confirmed attempt to target the Saudi capital during the current phase of fighting, according to the [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/yemen-saudi-arabia-b1286cad816dd3e553f50f6dd5205972).

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the group used ballistic and cruise missiles as well as drones. He claimed the operation targeted what he described as sensitive sites in Riyadh and facilities belonging to Saudi Aramco in Yanbu. The group also claimed that fires broke out, but it provided no independently verifiable evidence for that assertion.

The Associated Press said it could not independently verify the Houthi account. Saudi authorities did not report damage at the claimed targets, and Saudi Aramco had not publicly confirmed an impact at its Yanbu facilities at the time of reporting.

## Saudi Arabia reports multiple interceptions

Saudi officials said other attempted attacks were thwarted near Yanbu, Taif, Baysh and the Farasan Islands. The locations span western and southwestern Saudi Arabia, suggesting an effort to pressure both population centres and strategically important areas along the Red Sea.

Yanbu is a major energy and industrial hub on the Red Sea coast. It contains refining, petrochemical and export infrastructure, making any credible threat to the city important for energy markets and maritime security even when an attack causes no confirmed damage.

Residents in Riyadh reported hearing explosions associated with the interception. Images and accounts circulating online could not by themselves establish where debris landed or whether every reported sound was connected to the same missile. Official statements remain the clearest confirmation that an interception took place and that no casualties were recorded.

The distinction between confirmed events and claims is particularly important during fast-moving conflict. Armed groups and governments both use public statements to demonstrate capability, shape public perception and deter further action. Details such as the number of weapons launched, their intended targets and the effectiveness of air defences may take time to establish.

## Houthi statement and regional context

The Houthis, formally known as Ansar Allah, control Yemen's capital Sanaa and large parts of the country's north and west. The movement has developed a missile and drone arsenal during years of war and has previously targeted Saudi territory, shipping routes and sites elsewhere in the region.

[Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/middle-east/yemen-s-houthis-claim-missile-drone-attacks-on-saudi-capital/4062167) reported the Houthi statement announcing attacks on the Saudi capital. The movement framed the operation as part of its response to the wider regional conflict.

Saudi Arabia led a military coalition that intervened in Yemen in 2015 after the Houthis seized Sanaa and drove the internationally recognized government from much of the north. Years of fighting created one of the world's gravest humanitarian crises. Although front lines later became less active and Saudi-Houthi talks reduced cross-border attacks, Yemen has not reached a comprehensive political settlement.

The renewed exchange raises questions about whether those de-escalation efforts can hold. A sustained return to missile and drone attacks on Saudi cities could draw Riyadh more directly into military operations and further complicate diplomatic attempts to end Yemen's war.

## Risk to energy and Red Sea security

The attempted strikes also carry implications beyond the immediate military confrontation. Saudi energy facilities are central to global oil supply, while the Red Sea is a crucial route for commercial shipping between Asia, Europe and the Middle East.

Even unsuccessful attacks can affect risk assessments for airlines, shipping companies, insurers and energy traders. The effect depends on whether launches continue, whether infrastructure is damaged and how Saudi Arabia and its partners respond.

Yemen's internationally recognized government has separately warned that Houthi weapons pose a growing threat to Red Sea security. [Novexa previously reported](https://www.novexa.news/news/yemen-warns-houthi-threat-red-sea-security) that Yemeni officials were seeking stronger regional and international action against the movement's military capabilities.

For now, the confirmed facts are narrower than the competing public claims: Saudi Arabia says it intercepted a ballistic missile headed toward Riyadh and stopped additional attempted attacks, while the Houthis say they launched a broader operation against the capital and Yanbu. No deaths or verified damage from the Riyadh interception had been reported, and the Houthi claim of fires at Aramco sites remained unsubstantiated.

Further official disclosures, satellite imagery or independent reporting may clarify the scale and outcome of the operation. Until then, reports of damage should be treated as claims rather than established fact.
