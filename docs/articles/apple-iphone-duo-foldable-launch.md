# After Years of "Two Years Away," Apple Finally Unveils Its First Folding iPhone: The iPhone Duo

By Abdul Basit

Cupertino, California: For a device that's been rumored, leaked, delayed, and rumored again for years, Apple's first foldable phone finally, actually exists. At the company's "Surprise and Shine" event in Cupertino on Wednesday, September 9, Apple introduced the iPhone Duo, a book-style folding iPhone that plants the company's flag firmly in territory Samsung has occupied for years.

It's also a notably symbolic launch for another reason: the [iPhone Duo is the first marquee device unveiled under Apple's new CEO, John Ternus](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/09/apple-unveils-its-first-foldable-the-iphone-duo/). A foldable iPhone finally being unveiled, after years of "just two more years" reports, feels like exactly the kind of statement a new CEO would want to make early.

## What the Duo Actually Looks Like

Unfold it, and you're looking at a 7.6-inch Retina display, roughly tablet-sized, with a 5.4-inch outer screen for quick glances and everyday use when closed. Rather than mimicking the narrow, vertical-slab foldables that defined the category's early years, Apple went with a wider form factor, one designed to make watching video and using apps side-by-side feel more natural once unfolded.

That wider shape puts the Duo in direct competition with a growing range of foldables from other manufacturers. Apple's pitch, though, leans on its own ecosystem: the company described the Duo as "inspired by the versatility of iPad," and it's clearly been built to let people treat it like a mini tablet as much as a phone.

One detail that's likely to spark its own round of discussion: the Duo has Touch ID, but no Face ID. Owners can also unlock the device using an Apple Watch, but it's a notable departure from the Face ID-first approach familiar to users of Apple's recent flagship phones.

## Software Built Around Folding, Not Just Fitted to It

Apple has adapted iOS 27 for the Duo, with interface elements such as Wi-Fi controls repositioned to dock along the side when the phone is held vertically and unfolded. Multitasking gets a real upgrade too: users can run apps in split-screen, pair them together, or, in one of the more useful-sounding features, pin a video at the top of the screen while running a separate app in the bottom half.

Apple also built in support for using the Duo half-folded, so it can sit on a table on its own, hands-free.

Those changes matter because a folding phone needs to offer something beyond a bigger version of the same screen. Being able to keep a video visible while answering messages, or consult one app while working in another, gives the extra display space an everyday purpose. Whether those interactions feel effortless will be an important part of the experience once customers begin using the device.

## Pricing That Matches the Ambition

None of this is expected to come cheap. [Pre-event reporting from MacRumors, citing Bloomberg's Mark Gurman](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/09/iphone-duo-starts-256gb-of-storage/), pointed to a starting price of roughly $2,000 for the base 256GB configuration, climbing to around $3,000 for the highest-capacity model. Those figures were reported ahead of the presentation and should be distinguished from a confirmed retail price list.

At those reported prices, the Duo would sit firmly in premium territory. Buyers would be weighing the convenience of a phone that opens into a larger workspace against the cost of keeping their existing handset or choosing a conventional flagship. A new form factor alone does not settle that calculation; the software and day-to-day usefulness have to justify it too.

## A Small Slice of the Market, For Now

Foldables remain a niche corner of the smartphone world. TechCrunch's launch report cites research firm Counterpoint as estimating that folding phones account for less than 2% of overall smartphone shipments.

That's where Apple's entrance could shift things. The same report cites Counterpoint's forecast that Apple's new foldable could capture up to 25% of the foldable market by the end of the year. That is a projection, not an observed sales result, but it illustrates the expectations surrounding the company's first device in the category.

There is an important distinction between winning a large share of foldable sales and making foldables a mainstream choice. Apple could become a significant player within this segment while folding phones still represent only a small portion of the wider market. The reported premium pricing makes that distinction especially relevant.

## The End of a Very Long Wait

It's worth pausing on just how long this has been coming. For years, reports of an imminent Apple foldable have put the device just over the horizon. The launch turns that recurring speculation into a product people can finally assess on its design and capabilities.

That the device is now unveiled under a new CEO, with software adapted to its folding screen, suggests Apple wants this to feel less like a rumor finally confirmed and more like a new chapter for the iPhone. But an announcement is only the beginning of that test. Comfort in the hand, reliability over time and how well familiar apps use the larger display will matter beyond the launch presentation.

Whether the Duo becomes the device that helps pull foldables into the mainstream, or remains a premium niche product, will become clearer once it reaches customers' hands.
